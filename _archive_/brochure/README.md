## Design Instructions

Refer to the reference brochure in the `reference/` folder and study how it creates a visually compelling, editorial-quality brochure. Learn from its use of color, typography, photography, composition, and information hierarchy. 

Pay particular attention to the following techniques.

### 1. High-Contrast Geometric Color Panels

**The Technique:**
Instead of placing dark text on an endless white page, the brochure divides the canvas into bold, edge-to-edge squares, rectangles, and asymmetrical panels.

**Why It Works:**
Color blocks function as visual “rooms.” They group related ideDoe simage as, distinguish one topic from another, and create rhythm without relying on conventional underlined headings or excessive dividers.

### 2. Typography as Graphic Art

**The Technique:**
The brochure uses oversized display typography, strong changes in scale, and bold, ALL-CAPS sans-serif headlines such as “LEARN GLOBALLY. LEAD BOLDLY.”

**Why It Works:**
Typography functions as both language and imagery. Large headlines communicate the central message immediately, while smaller body text provides supporting detail for readers who choose to explore further.

### 3. Experiential, Authentic Human Photography

**The Technique:**
Instead of stiff corporate stock photography, the brochure uses candid images of students collaborating, presenting, building, exploring, and working in real-world environments.

**Why It Works:**
People connect with people and experiences, not abstract institutions. Action-oriented photography makes the program feel active, human, aspirational, and attainable.

### 4. Modular “Bento Box” Layouts

**The Technique:**
Information is divided into distinct cards, grids, timelines, tables, and side-by-side containers.

**Why It Works:**
Modular layouts eliminate walls of text. They turn complex information into a visual menu that readers can scan and explore in any order.

## Brand Typography System

Use the following typography consistently across every page and component.

### Primary Sans-Serif: Manrope

Manrope is the primary font for:

* Body text
* Labels and captions
* Navigation and UI-style elements
* Statistics and numerical callouts
* Buttons and calls to action
* Bold, ALL-CAPS section titles
* Large structural and campaign-style headlines

Use Manrope to create clarity, precision, modernity, and strong visual contrast.

### Serif Display Font: Newsreader

Newsreader is the editorial display font for:

* Editorial-style headings
* Narrative headlines
* Pull quotes
* Opening statements
* Founder or student quotations
* Reflective or emotionally expressive text
* Select large display phrases that require warmth and sophistication

Use Newsreader selectively. It should introduce an editorial, intellectual, and human character without weakening the brochure’s modern geometric structure.

### Fallback Fonts

Use the following fallback stacks:
Doe simage 
* Manrope fallback: `Manrope, Arial, sans-serif`
* Newsreader fallback: `Newsreader, Georgia, serif`

Do not introduce additional typefaces unless explicitly requested.

### Typographic Hierarchy

Maintain a clear distinction between structural and editorial typography:

* Use bold, ALL-CAPS Manrope for structural section titles, category labels, navigation markers, and high-impact campaign headlines.
* Use Newsreader for expressive headings, narrative statements, quotations, and editorial moments.
* Use Manrope for all body copy and functional information.
* Create extreme size contrast between display text and supporting copy.
* Never underline headings.
* Avoid using more than three substantially different font sizes within a single content module.

## Explicit Layout Instructions

### 1. Enforce Full-Bleed Geometric Panels

Do not default to a conventional white page surrounded by uniform margins.

Break each page or major screen into full-bleed geometric regions, such as:

* 2×2 grids
* 33/66 splits
* 40/60 splits
* Full-width horizontal bands
* Asymmetric editorial columns
* Interlocking card systems
* Image-and-text mosaics

Each major section must have a clearly defined background container.

Use oxblood, ivory white, and deep verdigris to establish hierarchy between these containers.

Ivory may provide negative space, but it must still feel intentionally composed rather than resembling an empty default document.

---

### 2. Mandate Extreme Typographic Contrast

Create a clear hierarchy between display text, headings, labels, and body copy.

* Use oversized **Newsreader** headings for editorial or intellectual statements.
* Use bold, uppercase **Manrope** for section labels, short hero declarations, graphic headings, statistics, and calls to action.
* Use Manrope for body copy and functional information.
* Make display text substantially larger than body copy.
* Keep line lengths controlled and readable.
* Never use underlined headings.
* Never introduce decorative fonts outside the approved type system.

Hero compositions may combine a small uppercase Manrope label with a large Newsreader headline.

---

### 3. Replace Continuous Text with Visual Containers

Never generate uninterrupted paragraphs longer than approximately three visual lines.

Break longer content into:

* Cards
* Pull quotes
* Numbered blocks
* Comparison tables
* Icon-supported modules
* Timelines
* Statistics
* Short editorial fragments
* Side notes
* Highlight panels

Every major concept should be enclosed within a distinct visual container or intentionally- Numerical callout and explanatory label

* Editorial quotation and attribution

Newsreader may be used for expressive hero lines, pull quotes, narrative headings, or editorial statements where a serif treatment strengthens the message.

Never use underlined headings.

Do not make every text element bold. Contrast must be created through scale, weight, spacing, typeface, and placement.

### 3. Replace Continuous Text with Visual Containers

Never generate a continuous paragraph longer than approximately three lines in the final layout.

Break longer ideas into:

* Cards
* Grid boxes
* Pull quotes
* Numbered steps
* Comparison tables
* Timelines
* Statistical callouts
* Icon-supported statements
* Short captioned modules

Every concept must be enclosed within a modular visual structure with clear separation from adjacent content.

Do not create dense pages that resemble reports, essays, or word-processing documents.

### 4. Embed Action-Oriented Photography as Structural Blocks

Treat photography as part of the page architecture, not as a floating illustration.

The user may provide an initial thought about the kind of image or layout they want. Use that as input, but do not follow it mechanically. Evaluate whether the proposed image truly fits the page’s purpose, tone, color palette, rhythm, and composition. If a different image treatment will serve the page better, prioritize the better fit and build toward that.

Photographs must:

* Sit flush against grid boundaries
* Fill their assigned panel
* Align precisely with adjacent text blocks
* Use intentional croppingDoe simage 
* Participate in the overall geometric composition

Prioritize images that feel unmistakably specific to the page:

* Ultra-photorealistic, not generic or stock-like
* Visually rich, tactile, and emotionally believable
* In direct conversation with the page’s theme
* Chosen for narrative fit, not just visual availability

Preferred subjects include:

* Candid people in real moments of collaborating, presenting, building, teaching, discussing, experimenting, or working in the field
* Real-world environments, skylines, buildings, workspaces, infrastructure, or context-setting scenes
* Abstract geometry only when it strengthens the page and supports the message

Avoid:

* Generic corporate handshakes
* Posed office portraits
* Artificial classroom scenes
* Obvious stock photography
* Images that feel inserted merely to fill space

The default goal is to make each image feel inevitable on that page, as if nothing better could have been placed there.

### 5. Preserve a Consistent Brand Palette

Every page must visibly belong to the same identity system.

Use oxblood, ivory white, and deep verdigris consistently throughout the brochure.

Do not assign colors randomly. Each color must have a clear compositional or informational purpose.

Oxblood should remain the most recognizable brand color. Deep verdigris should provide controlled contrast, while ivory white should preserve readability and visual breathing space.

### 6. Use Typography According to Function

Do not use the fonts interchangeably without purpose.

* Manrope communicates structure, clarity, action, and modernity.
* Newsreader communicates narrative, reflection, culture, and intellectual depth.
* Arial and Georgia are fallbacks only and should not be intentionally selected when the primary fonts are available.

All functional text, including labels, buttons, navigation, captions, and body copy, must use Manrope.

Use Newsreader only where an editorial or expressive treatment adds meaningful value.

### 7. Build Every Page Around One Dominant Message

Each page or spread must have one immediately recognizable takeaway.

A reader who views the page for only two seconds should still understand its main idea through:

* A dominant headline
* A large statistic
* A strong image
* A clear visual comparison
* A short editorial statement

Supporting details must remain subordinate to the primary message.

### 8. Maintain Editorial Restraint

The layout should feel bold but not chaotic.

Avoid:

* Excessive decoration
* Gratuitous gradients
* Random color changes
* Too many card styles
* Overuse of icons
* Unnecessary shadows
* Rounded containers without a clear design reason
* Decorative typography that conflicts with Manrope or Newsreader

Create sophistication through composition, proportion, typography, cropping, spacing, and disciplined use of the brand palette.

### 9. Treat Whitespace as an Active Design Element

Ivory-white space must be intentionally composed rather than treated as an empty leftover area.

Use whitespace to:

* Separate ideas
* Increase emphasis
* Improve reading flow
* Balance dense color panels
* Frame editorial typography
* Create moments of visual calm

Do not fill every available area with text, images, or decoration.

### 10. Ensure Cross-Page Visual Rhythm

Alternate between visually dense and visually quiet pages.

For example:

* Follow a bold oxblood hero page with an ivory editorial page.
* Follow a photography-heavy spread with a structured verdigris data page.
* Follow a modular information grid with a single large Newsreader quotation.

The brochure should feel like a deliberately paced visual narrative rather than a collection of unrelated templates.

## Final Design Standard

The finished brochure must feel:

* Bold
* Editorial
* Modern
* Intellectual
* Human
* Structured
* Sophisticated
* Visually confident

It must not resemble a conventional corporate report, a standard presentation deck, or a text-heavy document.

Every page should demonstrate deliberate use of:

* Full-bleed geometric composition
* Manrope and Newsreader typography
* Oxblood, ivory white, and deep verdigris
* Modular information design
* Authentic human photography
* Strong visual hierarchy
* Controlled editorial restraint
